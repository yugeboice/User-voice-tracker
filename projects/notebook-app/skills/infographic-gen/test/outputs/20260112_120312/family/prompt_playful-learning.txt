**a) What it is - Definition/Overview**
Colors are actually light waves of different sizes that our eyes and brain work together to decode. When light hits objects, some wavelengths bounce back to create the rainbow of colors we see every day.

**b) Why it matters - Relevance and Impact**

• Colors influence our feelings and behaviors in classrooms, bedrooms, and play spaces

**c) Key Points - 7 Most Important Concepts**

**• Light Waves Are Like Water Ripples**
Different sized invisible waves travel through air and space. When they hit objects, some bounce back to our eyes while others get absorbed. Example: A red apple absorbs all colors except red wavelengths.

**• Your Brain Creates Every Color You See**
Our eyes catch bouncing light waves and send secret messages to our brain, which translates them into colors. Pink doesn't exist in rainbows - our brain mixes red and white to create it!

**• Animals See Secret Colors We Can't**
Butterflies see ultraviolet patterns on flowers that are completely invisible to humans. Bulls can't see red at all - they're colorblind and chase the cape's movement, not its color!

**• Ancient People Had Different Color Words**
Egyptians used the same word for blue and green. Blue was so rare and expensive that only kings and queens could afford it - that's why we say "royal blue!"

**• You Can Make Rainbows at Home**
Water in a glass near sunlight bends light waves and separates them into hidden colors. Soap in milk makes colors dance by pushing fat molecules around!

**• Colors Change How You Feel**
Red makes us energetic and excited, blue helps us feel calm, and green relaxes our eyes. That's why many classrooms use soft green or blue paint!

**• All Colors Together Make White Light**
When you spin red, blue, and yellow circles really fast, they blend to appear white or gray, proving that white light contains every color hiding inside!

**d) How to Apply/Use**
Three simple experiments kids can try: rainbow-in-a-glass with water and sunlight, color-dancing milk with soap and food coloring, and spinning color wheels to see white light.
