## c) Key points

**Three Claimants, One Crown**
Edward the Confessor's death in January 1066 sparked a succession crisis with Harold Godwinson (crowned by English nobility), Harald Hardrada (Viking king claiming ancestral rights), and William of Normandy (claiming Edward's promise) all vying for power.

**The End of the Viking Age**
Harold's lightning 250-mile march north and decisive victory at Stamford Bridge on September 25th killed both Harald Hardrada and 15,000 Viking warriors, permanently ending Viking invasions of England.

**William's Strategic Invasion**
Just three days after Stamford Bridge, William landed at Pevensey Bay with 7,000 Norman soldiers, forcing exhausted Harold to march his depleted army 250 miles south for a second major battle.

**The Battle of Hastings Turning Point**
On October 14th, Harold's shield wall on Senlac Hill initially held against Norman cavalry, but rumors of William's death caused Anglo-Saxon formation breaks, leading to devastating Norman counterattacks and Harold's death.

**Language Revolution**
Norman French became the court language for 300+ years while Latin dominated administration, creating a trilingual society that eventually merged into enriched Middle English with French vocabulary.

**Architectural and Administrative Legacy**
William's Domesday Book (1086) created medieval Europe's most comprehensive social survey, while Norman stone castles and cathedrals transformed the English landscape permanently.

**Birth of Anglo-Norman Civilization**
The conquest didn't just change rulers - it created an entirely new hybrid culture that influenced European development and established England's complex continental relationships.
